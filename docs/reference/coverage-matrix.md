## Where the seven classes do not fit

`DEAD` was defined as *upstream gone or superseded*. 13 of the
18 units in it are not ham software — AHRL's own wallpapers,
menu tooling and version stamp, a browser, an editor, a system monitor,
a build toolchain, Wine, two scripts that fetch a web page. They are
marked `DEAD` with the note *drop* because the matrix was asked for
exactly seven classes; they are not dead upstreams, and a reader counting
dead ham software should subtract them. The 5 that remain
(dump1090, aa-analyzer, ESPHamClock, Open Wouxun (owx), Virtual Radar Server) are superseded in the
catalog, and only ESPHamClock's upstream is actually gone.

7 units are `DELTA_*` with no manifest
(Fritzing, KiCad, ngspice, GSpiceUI, FoxTelem, Morse Runner, Country files (cty.dat)). The electronics
tools and GSpiceUI are reserved to the maintainer (dispositions M),
FoxTelem and the country files are post-1.0 by Q-015, and Morse Runner
is a Windows binary. They are classified by what AHRL does with them,
not by what the catalog does.

5 units the archive carries are built or fetched by the
catalog anyway (fldigi, QLog, WSJT-X, WSJT-X improved, NanoVNA-Saver).
Each row's note says why; three of the manifests' reasons — that no
distribution packages NanoVNA-Saver, that only Kali packages QLog, that
the archive's WSJT-X is the only one — are claims this probe
contradicts, and those manifests owe a correction.

## What the blend as base tier leaves to Hammunition

With the blend installed as the base tier, 37 of AHRL's 95 units are already on the machine and 15 more are one `apt install` away — together 52 of the 77 units that are not `DEAD`, or 67% — if the operator installs all twelve task metapackages, which is what *base tier* would mean. That is the measured version of the survey's claim that the blend makes most of AHRL redundant, and it holds. What it leaves is 25 units: 17 source builds the archive does not carry (Coil64, cwwav, flaa, flcluster, fllog, Fl_MoxGen, flnet, flwkey, glfer, gsmc, GSpiceUI, Linrad, MSHV, qgrid, xwefax, not1mm, AIS-catcher), 8 upstream binaries and no proprietary tools. Plus the 8 stale rows, where the operator who wants AHRL's version needs a build the blend cannot give them. Hammunition's actual scope on the amateur side is therefore not a catalog of 77 programs but a build layer for roughly 33 of them — the W1HKJ suite, the Qt and FLTK one-offs, the Python venvs — sitting on a base someone else maintains; and the tiering, hardware, station configuration and SDR/SIGINT profiles that no metapackage attempts. The 18 `DEAD` units are the explanation AHRL never wrote down.
